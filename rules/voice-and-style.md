# Pallav's voice and style

## Two registers

- **In chat with you** Pallav is casual and friendly, "bro"-level informal. You can be relaxed back in your working messages to him.
- **In published content** the voice is different: explanatory, first person, coherent full sentences, British spelling. Do not carry chat slang into public copy.

## The published voice

**It sounds like him explaining something out loud.** "Let me tell you about the most important number. Runway." He defines a concept, then applies it, then shows what it means for you. Think of him talking to a younger cousin: plain, earnest, warm, Indian context.

Do:
- Write coherent full sentences that follow from one another. Each line answers the question the previous line raised.
- Define every concept before using it. Give the full context; never assume the reader already knows the idea.
- Speak to one person ("you"). Use first person for proof ("When I quit my 9to5, people said I was ruining my career.").
- Use concrete Indian anchors: ₹, lakh, salary, hike, EMI, manager, appraisal, Sunday-night dread.
- Keep qualifiers honest ("for many", "often", "rarely"). Avoid "never" and "always" unless he said it.
- Close with a thought the reader can keep or a question to ask themselves, then the CTA.

Don't:
- Write crisp, clipped, fragment lines ("Spend less. Save more. Quit sooner."). Pallav rejected reel 004's first draft as "too crisp and incoherent" for this reason.
- Sound like a textbook, a poster, or a motivational slogan.
- Use the banned patterns (from the blog review and card rubric):
  - "X, not Y", "It's not X, it's Y", "isn't X. It's Y", "You don't need X. You need Y"
  - em dashes or en dashes in the text
  - "Here's the thing", "Let that sink in", lifeboat/anchor imagery, triple-metaphor slogans
  - AI words: delve, tapestry, testament, pivotal, robust, seamless, powerful, cutting-edge, leverage, utilise, foster, embark, realm, landscape, comprehensive, crucial, paramount, game-changer, revolutionise, unlock, unleash, harness, furthermore, moreover, navigate, "it's worth noting", "in today's fast-paced world"
- Hype, ragebait, fake urgency, "10x". This is not a rage-quit book: the message is to build value, become hard to replace, then set the terms of your work.

## Format-specific notes

| Format | Voice note |
|---|---|
| Reel voiceover | Full sentences, spoken rhythm, at most two spoken numbers. `9to5` is written as "nine-to-five" only in the TTS input. |
| Instagram / LinkedIn caption | Restates the idea as a claim in line 1, then explains more fully than the reel, with a worked example or 2–3 practical steps. |
| Threads | The same explanation compressed to 500 characters or fewer. |
| Blog | Personal hook in 1–3 lines, question H2s, the book framework introduced as "In my upcoming book, *Quit 9to5*, I…", a thought exercise at the end. |
| Cards | One idea, 12–40 words, the payoff in the last few words, closes the loop ("so what do I do?"). |

## Spelling and typography

British spelling throughout (per cent, optimising, practise as a verb, learnt). Indian number formatting (₹12 lakh, ₹6,00,000). Straight apostrophes in blog source. No ellipsis character in blog copy.
