# Instagram reel scripting: retention-first method

A reel is judged second by second. People decide in the first 3 seconds whether to stay, then whether to finish, then whether to send it to someone. This method writes for that curve. It is generic: plug in the brand's own playbook (voice, style, facts, mandatory CTA) where marked **[BRAND]**.

Research basis (checked Nov 2026, re-verify every few months because the platform changes):
- Instagram's top ranking signals are **watch time, likes per reach and sends per reach**; sends matter slightly more for reaching non-followers (Adam Mosseri, 2025–2026). Judge reels by rates, not raw views.
- Reel insights list **skip rate** (people who leave in the first ~3 s) first in order of importance, then share rate. Meta's Reels ranking predicts "watched < 3 s" and "watched more than 95% of viewers" (relative, not absolute).
- Instagram's own guidance: hook in the first 3 seconds; make the reel make sense to people who don't follow you; first-frame text works like a headline; try a question in the first frame and the answer a few seconds later; avoid clickbait and engagement bait; original content only (reposts lose recommendations).
- No ideal length. Instagram counts both % watched and seconds watched. Shorter is easier to hold to the end; reels over 3 minutes aren't recommended to non-followers.
- Creator-educator consensus (not platform data): one idea per video; one open loop, paid off at the end; a visual change every 2–4 s; hook text on screen in frame 1 because many people watch muted; end on the payoff, not a goodbye; a last frame that mirrors the first invites a rewatch.

Sources: socialmediatoday.com (Mosseri, Jan 2025, Feb 2025, May 2026), creators.instagram.com (blog Feb 2025, FAQ, originality Apr 2026), about.instagram.com (Reels guide, Sep 2026), buffer.com/resources/instagram-algorithms, plus short-form craft guides (sparround.com, shortzly.com, goscript.app).

## Inputs to gather first
1. **[BRAND] playbook:** voice, banned words, visual style, caption zone, mandatory CTA (exact words), compliance lines (e.g. "not financial advice").
2. **[BRAND] fact base:** the book, posts, or notes the claims must come from. Never invent facts, quotes or statistics. A number is either sourced or clearly labelled as an example.
3. **The goal of the reel:** reach new people (optimise for sends), or deepen followers (likes, saves).
4. **The viewer:** one specific person and the one thing they want.

## Step 1: Find the ONE idea
- Write it as a single sentence a friend could repeat: "X does Y, so Z."
- If you need "and also", it's two reels. Cut the second idea or make a series.
- Prefer an idea with a **mechanism** (why it happens) and a **felt cost** (what it costs the viewer), because those get sent.
- Send test: would the viewer forward this to a specific friend with "this is you"? If not, sharpen the cost or the surprise.

## Step 2: Pick the hook (0–3 s)
Write 5–8 hooks, keep the best, keep 2 as alternates for a Trial Reel test. Each hook has a **spoken line** and a **first-frame headline (4–7 words)**. They share the job; the screen is not a word-for-word copy.

Hook types that work for voiceover reels:
| Type | Shape |
|---|---|
| Goal hook | "Want to [viewer's goal]? [Instruction / check] first." |
| Numbered promise (open loop) | "[Thing] does two things to you." |
| Hidden cost | "Your [everyday thing] could cost you [specific outcome]." |
| First-person stance | "I [unusual choice]. Here's why it mattered." |
| Negation | "Stop [common habit] if you want [goal]." |
| Question → answer later | Question in frame 1, answer a few seconds in |

Rules: no greeting, no "in this video", no slow fade-in. The first frame is already moving and already shows the stake (one strong object or image). Avoid clickbait formulas ("you won't believe…", "this will shatter…"); they win the click and lose the send.

## Step 3: Build the chain (beat sheet)
Write one line per beat. **Each line answers the question the previous line raises.** Read it aloud and say the question between lines ("why?", "what's the second?", "so what?"). If a line doesn't answer the last one, reorder or bridge it.

Default arc for a 30–40 s voiceover reel (about 8–10 beats of 3–4 s):
| Beat | Time | Job |
|---|---|---|
| Hook | 0–3 s | Goal or stake, plus a checkable claim. Opens the loop. |
| Loop set | 3–7 s | Make the promise explicit ("two things", "the one nobody counts"). Show a counter or placeholder on screen. |
| Easy payoff | 7–11 s | Deliver the expected part fast, which buys trust. |
| Re-hook | ~10–14 s | Pattern interrupt plus an escalation ("this is the one nobody counts"). The mid-video dip starts here. |
| Reveal | 14–18 s | The surprise, in one short, rhythmic sentence (the send-worthy line). |
| Mechanism | 18–22 s | "So…": one moving number or one cause. |
| Payoff | 22–26 s | Close the hook's loop with a felt cost or benefit. Slow the read a little. |
| Proof + action | 26–31 s | First-person proof or one usable takeaway. Visually mirror the first frame (e.g. the lock opens). |
| CTA | last 5–6 s | **[BRAND]** mandatory CTA, the same every time. Hold the end frame ~0.4 s so the loop back to frame 1 reads as intentional. |

Loop discipline: **one** main open loop per reel, always paid off. The payoff comes after about 70% of the runtime, and nothing new is introduced after it except the CTA.

## Step 4: Write the words
- **Word budget:** about 2.5 spoken words per second. 25–30 s ≈ 60–75 words; 30–38 s ≈ 75–95 words, including the CTA. Cut from the middle, never from the hook or the payoff.
- **Numbers:** speak at most two (or one comparison like "two years becomes almost four"). Every other figure goes on screen. Recalculate every example and show the exact figure on screen.
- Short sentences, plain words, second person for the viewer, first person for proof. Concrete local context (currency, everyday objects) beats abstractions.
- Avoid absolutes ("never", "always") and don't sermonise. One honest nuance can live in the caption or as small on-screen text.
- Compliance lines (disclaimers, "example") go on screen, not in the voiceover.

## Step 5: Design the picture per beat
For each beat, write: **statement text** (1–2 short lines, one highlighted word), **one illustrated object** (one main element on screen), and **the motion that lands on the spoken word**.
- Change something every 2–4 s: a new object, a counter tick, a hard cut, a highlight swipe, a number roll.
- The picture does the arithmetic; the voice says what it means.
- The reel must work muted: the headline in frame 1 plus burned-in captions carry the whole story.
- Use one recurring object across beats (a lock, a jar, a door) so the ending can mirror the opening.
- Keep text inside safe zones; leave the right edge and the bottom clear for the platform UI.

## Step 6: Caption
- Line 1 restates the idea as a claim (the caption preview is a second hook).
- Add value the video didn't have room for: the worked example in full, the nuance, 2–3 practical actions.
- Plain text, short paragraphs, no link unless the brand allows it. End with the **[BRAND]** CTA line.
- No engagement bait ("comment YES").

## Step 7: Review gate (run before handing over)
- [ ] One idea, sayable in one sentence
- [ ] Hook in ≤ 3 s, spoken and on screen in frame 1; first frame already moving
- [ ] Makes sense to someone who has never seen the account
- [ ] Each line answers the one before (read aloud with the questions in between)
- [ ] Exactly one open loop, set early, paid off after ~70% of runtime
- [ ] A re-hook or pattern interrupt around 10–14 s; visual change every 2–4 s
- [ ] ≤ 2 spoken numbers; all maths rechecked; examples labelled
- [ ] Every claim traced to the **[BRAND]** fact base; no invented stats
- [ ] Word count fits the target length at ~2.5 words/s
- [ ] Works muted
- [ ] Send test passed: there's a line someone would forward
- [ ] Ends on payoff → CTA → held frame that mirrors or loops to frame 1
- [ ] Mandatory CTA exact, in voiceover, captions and end card
- [ ] Original content (own visuals, own voice); no reposted clips or watermarks

## Step 8: Deliver
Deliver: the one-sentence idea; a beat table (time, voiceover, on-screen text, object and motion, retention job); the clean voiceover block for recording; the caption; 2 alternative hooks; and the sources for any facts. After posting, read skip rate, average watch time and sends per reach, and feed what you learn into the hook list.

## Platform notes: don't import other platforms' habits
- LinkedIn writing lessons that transfer: strong first-person stances, concrete detail in line 2, tight chain of thought, avoiding clickbait.
- LinkedIn habits that don't transfer: the "…more" fold, long text bodies, whitespace formatting, links in the first comment, optimising for dwell time on text. Instagram is visual, often muted, scored second by second, and grows through sends.
