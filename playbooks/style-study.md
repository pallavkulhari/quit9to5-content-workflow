- "Everyone wants "fuck you" money. / No one wants to be told "fuck you" on their way to getting it." (12.2K)
- "You aren't achieving your big goals because you aren't willing to sacrifice your small goals." (5.0K)

- "If you're not sure about whether to keep a leader, imagine what would happen if they were gone. If the answer is "nothing", then that's probably what they're doing." (1.7K)
Patterns:
- **Mirrored pair:** the same words get flipped in line 2 (wants money / told fuck you; big goals / small goals).
- **Length:** usually one or two sentences of 12–30 words.
- **Ending:** a twist on the last word or clause. The payoff comes at the end ("...that's probably what they're doing").
- **Thought experiments:** "imagine if they were gone" turns a concept into a test the reader can run.
- **Vocabulary:** plain, blunt words and some swearing. No metaphors stacked on top of each other.

Patterns:
- **"Your ___" opener** that speaks straight to the reader's brain, habits or circle.
- **Claim, mechanism, instruction.** It runs to 2–4 sentences (30–60 words), with a short imperative at the end.
- **Short aphorisms that do best:** five to seven words.
- **Uses "X isn't Y, it's Z" a lot.** His biggest post ("isn't fear of commitment. It's commitment...") does this. We skip this pattern on purpose, because Pallav bans it.

## What carries over to Quit 9to5 cards
1. One idea per card. Best at 12–30 words, with a ceiling of about 40.
2. Put the punch in the last 3–6 words.
3. Use concrete anchors: ₹, LPA, EMI, hike, manager, Sunday, Monday.
4. Superlatives ("The most ___") and thought experiments ("Count / Calculate / Imagine") work without any context.
5. Mirrored pairs are allowed only when both halves are concrete. Never use the "X, not Y" slogan form.
6. No backstory. A stranger should get it in three seconds.
